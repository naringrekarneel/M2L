CREATOR'S NOTE • THE STORY BEHIND M2L

Why did I make M2L?

A personal reflection on overcoming tutorial overload, infinite algorithmic distraction, and building a calm tool for self-directed learners.

“

Like many self-taught developers, engineers, and curious students, I spent years bookmarking 60-video YouTube playlists and saving 10-hour mega-courses into my "Watch Later" list — only to watch them collect digital dust.

YouTube is an incredible repository of human knowledge, but it is fundamentally engineered for continuous consumption, not structured graduation. When you open a playlist to study, you are immediately bombarded by recommendation rabbit holes, distracting comments, and an overwhelming counter showing "Video 1 of 87" with no idea when you'll actually finish.

I built M2L (Map to Learn) because I needed a tool that turned chaotic video dumps into a clear, predictable daily roadmap. I didn't want another complex subscription platform, a bloated SaaS dashboard, or an app that locks your data behind a login wall. I wanted something clean, honest, and laser-focused on one job: telling me exactly what to watch today, and keeping me honest until graduation.

The 3 Non-Negotiable Rules of M2L

  • 01. Zero Cloud Leaks: No accounts, no passwords, no email popups, and no tracking cookies. Everything you check off lives 100% locally inside your browser's local storage.
  • 02. Calibrated Pacing: Whether you give it an 80-video playlist or a 12-hour single video, it splits lessons evenly across your target graduation date with direct timestamp jumps.
  • 03. Guilt-Free Backlog: Life happens. If you miss a day, modules don't trigger red penalty warnings; they safely quarantine into the Backlog Vault so you can catch up calmly at your own pace.

I hope M2L gives you the calm focus you need to finish whatever skill you're learning right now. If it helps you cross the finish line of even one playlist that you had given up on, building this was 100% worth it.

NN
